# Name check: Inbox Bodyguard

**Recommendation: keep "Inbox Bodyguard".** No product registered under that exact name turned up. It says what the product does in two plain words, and it gives the demo a story: an assistant with a bodyguard.

## What the quick search found (web search, 25 Oct 2026)
| Finding | Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ScamBlock** (scamblock.net) uses the tagline *"Your Personal Inbox Bodyguard"* for an on-device Gmail phishing scanner | Low–medium | It uses the phrase as a tagline, not a product name, but it is the same category. Don't use "Your personal inbox bodyguard" as our tagline. |
| **Bodyguard / Bodyguard.ai** (Bodyguard SAS, Nice, FR): live USPTO marks **BODYGUARD** (Reg. 7459703) and **BODYGUARD.AI** (Reg. 7470851), classes 9 & 42, for AI content moderation | Medium if commercialised | Same classes and an adjacent space (AI safety/moderation). Fine for a hackathon. Before going commercial, get trademark advice; the compound "Inbox Bodyguard" may still conflict. Never shorten our name to just "Bodyguard". |
| Generic usage ("your inbox's bodyguard") in blog posts (TempoMailUSA, PitchSlap, Total Defense, a "Defend" LinkedIn post) | Low | A descriptive phrase is hard to own exclusively. That's another reason to keep the full two-word name. |

This was a quick search, not legal clearance.

## Usage
- Write it **Inbox Bodyguard**: two words, both capitalised. The slug is `inbox-bodyguard`.
- Never shorten it to "Bodyguard" (that's the trademark above). If you need a short internal name for the guard feature, use **"the guard"** in lowercase.
- In UI copy the AI is "the assistant". The safety layer is "the guard".

## Taglines (pick one; the first is primary)
1. **The AI email assistant that can't be turned against you.** (primary; used on the presentation board)
2. An AI assistant for your inbox, with a bodyguard. (landing H1)
3. Your assistant reads everything. The guard reads it first.
4. Remembers your context. Refuses their instructions.

Devpost one-liner: *Inbox Bodyguard is a personal AI email assistant with memory and real actions, protected by a NVIDIA Nemotron guard model that catches prompt injections hidden in emails and shows what it blocked, why, and which model decided.*
